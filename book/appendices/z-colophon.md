The animal on the cover of *Publishing with Press* is a common cuttlefish (*Sepia officinalis*). It lives in the coastal waters of the eastern Atlantic and the Mediterranean. It reaches 45 centimeters in mantle length at most, and most adults are smaller. A common cuttlefish lives for one to two years. It has eight arms and two longer feeding tentacles. Its eye has a W-shaped pupil.

The cuttlefish changes color in moments through chromatophores, small sacs of pigment in the skin that muscles open and close. Under threat, it releases a cloud of dark ink. The pigment sepia was made from that ink. The pigment and the genus share the Greek name for the cuttlefish.

Pandoc and LuaLaTeX set the Markdown manuscript in Libertinus Serif, with Source Code Pro for code, under press 3.0.2, the press it describes. Scripts kept with the manuscript drew the diagrams with Pillow and matplotlib. The plates were commissioned from an image model. The press's own intake took them in, and `press art enhance` finished them. The cover engraving came from an image model too, and the logomark from Gemini. A script kept with the manuscript keyed the engraving and composed the cover.

Language models drafted the text under direction from a fact base drawn from the press source and its documentation. A group of editorial agents then checked the text against that fact base.

The cover design follows the tradition of the engraved-animal technical manual: one animal, engraved in line, beneath a band of solid color. The title is set in Libertinus Serif condensed to 82 percent, and the subtitle in Libertinus Serif Italic. The band is sepia. It carries the series line, *A Workbench Handbook*, in white small capitals. The engraving beneath the title shows the cuttlefish alone, facing left, with no scenery.

# Fact check: Chapter 5, Figures, plates, and illustrations

I checked `book/chapters/05-figures-and-plates.md` against `docs/briefs/facts/facts-05.md`, which covers sections 4.2, 10.7, 8.5, 8.6, 8.7, and 9.5, plus the Shared context (1.4, 19, 20). Line numbers refer to the chapter. One ruling from the managing editor also applies. The editor tested it: a comment on the line right after an image suppresses the figure. The form image, blank line, comment is correct and is now the book's rule.

**Captured output.** The chapter quotes two verbatim blocks. The orphan-plate message (line 119) matches the file character for character. The guardrail block (lines 65 to 69) has the right words but different line breaks (Drifted 1). Example 5.5 elides every command's output, and its commands match the documented grammar: `press illustrate <name>`, `press art accept <file> --as plate:<name>` (the same command as the hint that illustrate prints), and `press art enhance [file]`. Examples 5.1 to 5.4 are inputs, not captures.

**Cross-references.** Every chapter title the chapter cites (6, 9, 11, and "the next chapter") matches the current chapter headings.

**Checked and supported** (not counted): the kind classes and Table 5.1; plates versus numbered figures and "Figure C.N"; v3.0.0 as the version that split them; `decorative=true`; the four fields; `art:`/`data:`/`source:`; the illustrate lookup by file stem, its three refusals, and the #225 message; the style precedence; the refusal of `decorative` together with `fig-alt`; the fifteen `press figures` fields, in order; the `\hyperref[...]{Figure~8.1}` form; no pandoc-crossref; an unknown `@id` left raw; no chapter or section references; the List of Plates condition; the orphan rule; latexmk; the toc-depth scar; hypcap and the footnote; the seven placements and Table 5.2; the parity-aware refusals and Table 5.3; the A.11 hedges on width, `margin`, and `full-bleed`; `\captionof` and `\cleardoubleevenpage`; the outset default; Needspace, wrapfigure `{i|o}`, `0.4\linewidth`, and Table 5.4; the reflowable editions; the alpha master; `\PressFigureCap`, `\linewidth`, "never up", and `\pandocbounded`; Table 5.5's five values and the A.5 hedge; the asset paths; the plate-name pattern; the JPEG-master scar and its quote; the four steps of `press art enhance` and the amd64/arm64 split; "no minimum pixel or DPI check"; 6.3 x 300 = 1890; the 1024 square; the print-safe caps, q88, `build/print-assets/`, and `print-safe-images.lua`; the Lulu story, 1024/1.7 = 602, and the bold "**neither**" quote; the `tex/title-page-print.tex` advice; and the "literal, silly plates" footnote. The Carroll details are right: Chapter 6 is "Pig and Pepper", Chapter 7 is "A Mad Tea-Party", Chapter 8 is "The Queen's Croquet-Ground", and the Cheshire Cat lines are quoted accurately.

## 1. Unsupported

1. **Claims about what the Alice example contains.** I searched for Alice, white-rabbit, croquet, tea-party, and cheshire. The file says nothing about the Alice example's plates, figures, or directories. Facts-04 raised the same item. The claims:
   - Line 18: "The examples come from the Alice example, which user `alice` keeps on the workstation `hatter`." If the `alice`/`hatter` persona is a convention of this book, it is fine. "The examples come from the Alice example" reads as a statement that these image lines exist in it.
   - Line 44: "Example 5.1 shows a plate for the first chapter of the Alice example."
   - Line 83: "a numbered map from Chapter 8 of the Alice example".
   - Line 166: "Example 5.3 gives the Alice example a frontispiece before Chapter 7".
   - Line 221: "Example 5.4 puts the Cheshire Cat on the fore-edge of a page in Chapter 6".
   - Line 292: "The Alice example keeps all its plates in that directory".

   Fix: frame them as illustrations ("Example 5.1 shows how a plate for the first chapter of the Alice example might be typed"), or have the editor confirm them against the example repository. For line 292, keep only what the file says: "the plates are PNG files under *assets/woodcuts/*".
2. **The sidebar's origin claim.** Line 279: "Press began as the build of one book, and that book once had a figure taller than its text block." I searched for one book, first book, began, and extracted. War story 1 says only "A figure taller than the text block makes LuaLaTeX ship empty pages forever". Fix: "A book built with press once had a figure taller than its text block." The claim is probably true, because the press's own CLAUDE.md says it. If the editor wants to keep it, add it to the fact file first.
3. **The default placement.** Line 134: "An image with no `place` sits in the run of the text, as `inline` does." I searched for default, inline, and place. The file gives defaults for the measure (`0.4\linewidth`), the outset (`1em`), and the guard (11), but no default `place`. Fix: cut it, or have the editor confirm it against `figures.py` or `figure-numbering.lua`.
4. **The source form of a wrap.** Line 221: "In the source, type the image line, one blank line, and then the paragraph that wraps, with nothing between them." The file says only "The wrap is fused onto the head of the next paragraph so text really runs around it." The blank line, and the claim that a comment between the wrap and its paragraph stops the fusion, come from the writer's own pandoc test (report-05, Concern 2), not from the file. Fix: "The filter fuses the wrap onto the head of the paragraph that follows it." Alternatively, have the managing editor test the claim, as they tested the comment rule, and add it to the book's rules. See also Missing 2.
5. **Section numbers.** Line 106: "The section numbers in the PDF come from pandoc, but no `@` form reaches them." I searched for section and number. The file says only that "There is no chapter or section cross-reference mechanism." Whether the house PDF numbers sections at all is not in this file. Fix: "No `@` form reaches a chapter or a section."
6. **The print interior and placement.** Line 240: "the PDF honors every placement in Table 5.2, and the print interior follows the same rules." The file speaks of "What each placement does in the PDF" and does not mention the print interior. Fix: cut "and the print interior follows the same rules", or have the editor confirm it (the print build runs `print-safe-images.lua`, so it does run filters, but the file does not say it runs `figure-numbering.lua`).
7. **The square plate meets the line first.** Line 260: "A square plate on the house page meets the line first." The file never gives the house `\linewidth`, so this cannot be checked. It is true if the line is shorter than 6.3in. Fix: "On most pages a square plate meets the line first", or give the line length from a source.
8. **The logo's filename in the print assets.** Line 340, the tip: "point the logo at *build/print-assets/press-logo.png*." The file says only "A custom `tex/title-page-print.tex` should point its logo at `build/print-assets/`." It does not name the file. Fix: "point the logo at its copy in *build/print-assets/*".
9. **Minor glosses not in the file.** These are low stakes. Soften them or accept them as general knowledge; the editor decides.
   - Table 5.1, `.photo`: "a photograph or a screenshot". The file gives no meaning for any class.
   - Line 59: "it ends at the first `-->`". This is HTML comment behaviour, not stated.
   - Sidebar, line 281: "a second rule for the hand-written title page". The file says only "never stack a third `titlepage`".
   - Line 210: "A wider wrap holds more text lines beside it and needs more free lines below its top." The file gives the guards but no reason for them. Also, a wider wrap leaves less text beside it, not more. The intended point is that a wider figure is taller. Fix: "A wider wrap is taller and needs more free lines below its top", or cut the reason.

## 2. Contradicted

1. **The comment on the next line (managing editor's ruling).** Line 59: "Press looks for the comment only after the image. It may sit on the next line or after one blank line." This repeats the file (4.2: "It may sit on the next line or after one blank line (`figures.py:93-99`)"), but the managing editor's test shows the next-line form breaks the figure. Pandoc then puts the image and the comment in one paragraph and makes no figure of it, so the image gets no caption, no number, and no List of Plates entry. The chapter offers that form to the reader as an equal choice. The tip at line 78 makes this worse: `press figures` still reports the directive in the next-line form, so the report the tip recommends will not reveal the fault. Fix, line 58: "Press reads a comment on the next line or after one blank line, but always leave the blank line. A comment on the very next line joins the image's paragraph. Pandoc then makes no figure of the image, and it loses its caption, its number, and its entry in the list. `press figures` still reports the directive, so the report will not show the fault." Add to the tip at line 78: "...and a comment directly under the image line, with no blank line, breaks the figure even though the report shows its directive." The fact file's own canonical example (4.2 and 8.5, the compositor) uses the broken form and should be corrected at the source.
2. **"Every edition" includes the Markdown edition.** 20.B.9: "The Markdown edition is raw stitched source. `::: verse` divs, figure attributes and `<!-- art: -->` comments ship as written." No filter runs on it, so no number is computed. Three claims run into this:
   - Line 100: "The citation prints as "Figure 8.1" in every edition, with a link to the figure."
   - Line 242: "The number and the citation survive in every edition".
   - Line 253, the tip: "the `@fig:` citation, which every edition resolves to the right number and link."

   The writer deliberately left the Markdown edition out of the list at line 240 (report-05). That is sound, but these three sentences still say "every". Fix: "in every typeset edition", or "in every edition except the Markdown edition, which is the raw manuscript".
3. **"Every image" in the print-safe pass.** Line 324: "The print build therefore passes every image through a *print-safe* pass." Table 5.6: "The pass resamples every interior figure and plate". 20.B.2 (unverified): "`print_safe` copies only the logo, top-level `assets/*.jpg` and `assets/woodcuts/*`", while the Lua filter redirects *all* `assets/...` images. An image at `assets/fig/x.jpg` may therefore be missing from the print interior. Line 292 ("Every image file lives under *assets/*") makes a subdirectory such as `assets/fig/` look safe. B says not to present the gap as a feature. The chapter instead states the opposite as fact. Fix: "The pass takes the logo, the top-level images in *assets/*, and everything in *assets/woodcuts/*." Then advise readers to keep interior images in *assets/woodcuts/*, as this book does, without explaining why in terms of the unverified bug.

## 3. Drifted

1. **The guardrail is reflowed and over-applied.** Lines 65 to 69 present the text as the exact string that press appends. The file's capture (8.5) is one line: `Single ink only, no colour wash. No text, no words, no lettering, no caption, no page border, no watermark, no signature.` The chapter breaks it into three lines, so it no longer matches the file character for character, and a reader could take the line breaks as part of the prompt. Fix: set it as one line, which the code block may wrap visually, or say in the lead-in that the lines are wrapped for the page. Also, line 63 says "Press adds a guardrail of its own to every commission." The file documents the guardrail only for `press illustrate` (8.5, "always appended"). Fix: "to every `press illustrate` commission".
2. **Figure 5.3 disagrees with the prose and with itself.** The `fig-alt` (line 244) says "One image line at the top branches to five editions". The directive draws four boxes, and those boxes stand for six editions (PDF, EPUB, HTML, reader, text, DOCX). Both the directive and the `fig-alt` also give the text and DOCX editions only "in-flow figure", with no number. Line 242 says "The number and the citation survive in every edition", and the file says the number appears "elsewhere, a link `Figure C.N`". Fix: in the `fig-alt`, "branches to four groups of editions". Give the text/DOCX box "Figure 8.1" as well, or have the editor confirm that those editions drop the label.
3. **The pypdf verifier "fails" the PDF.** Line 130: "The PDF verifier reads the link destinations with pypdf and fails the PDF when an entry points at the wrong place." War story 5 says only "When touching PDF links, verify destinations with pypdf... The verifier now does so." Fix: "The PDF verifier now checks the link destinations with pypdf."
4. **"Nothing came out."** The sidebar, line 279: "Nothing in the output looked like a failure, because nothing came out that anyone could inspect." The file says LuaLaTeX "ship[s] empty pages forever, silently". The fault is silence, and empty pages are output. Fix: "Nothing reported a failure: the run never finished, so there was no PDF to inspect", or cut the sentence.
5. **"1.7 inches wide."** Line 335. The file (10.7) says "a 1024px logo at 1.7in". It does not say which dimension. Fix: "printed at 1.7 inches".
6. **The cap and enlargement.** Line 288: "The cap also explains why the house cannot enlarge a small image." The cap is a ceiling, and the never-up rule is separate ("scaled down to fit ... and never up"). Fix: "The scaling rule has a second consequence: press never enlarges a small image."
7. **The source of "the authoritative reading".** Line 75: "The press documentation calls this report". The file gives the quotation with no source. This is a minor point. Fix: "Press calls this report".

## 4. Missing

1. **The Markdown edition ships the image lines raw** (20.B.9): "figure attributes and `<!-- art: -->` comments ship as written." This is the chapter's subject, and the reader should know that their art directions are published verbatim in one edition. Line 240 is the place for it. State it as a fact, not as a feature.
2. **A wrapped plate cannot carry its directive.** This follows from Unsupported 4, if the editor confirms it. A comment between a wrap and its paragraph stops the fusion. A comment after the paragraph is more than one blank line from the image (4.2). So a wrapped plate has no `art:` directive, and `press illustrate` refuses it with "A caption is a label, not art direction (#225)" unless the reader passes `--subject`. Example 5.4 has no directive and does not explain why. One sentence after Example 5.4 would do: "A wrap cannot carry a directive, so commission a wrapped plate with `press illustrate <name> --subject "..."`."
3. **The 7.1in figure in the skills** (20.A.5): "The cover-design and plates skills say 7.1in." Line 262 says 6.3 "is the figure you will most often see quoted". A reader of the plates skill will see 7.1 and wonder which is right. One clause would settle it. Also, the file says v2 and later have six profiles (A.1) but gives caps for five. Table 5.5's "the profiles that the header names" covers the gap, but the editor may want the sixth cap.
4. **What `press art enhance` does to file names** (8.7): "Without a file argument, it finishes every `assets/woodcuts/*.jpg|*.png`. Output always goes to `<stem>.png`." Running it on a JPEG plate leaves the JPEG on disk beside a new PNG, and the orphan rule at line 116 would then likely refuse the unreferenced file. The orphan consequence is my inference, so check it first. The no-argument form is worth one clause in the paragraph at line 312.
